About

Welcome to my personal page!

I am a first-year Ph.D. student at the Shanghai Institute for Mathematics and Interdisciplinary Sciences (hereafter, SIMIS) at Fudan University. Under the supervision of Prof. Bingjie Li, my research focuses on developing mathematical, statistical, and computational methods for analyzing high-dimensional -omics data, with a particular emphasis on single-cell -omics. I am currently involved in several large-scale projects; for more information, please see the Projects tab.

Prior to coming to Shanghai, I was a bioinformatician in the Breuss Lab in the Department of Pediatrics at the University of Colorado School of Medicine, where I worked on projects involving the simulation of mosaic variants in humans and the analysis of single-cell ATAC and RNA sequencing data in trisomy 21 (Down syndrome).

I was born in Baoding, China, and lived there until I was ten. I then grew up all over the United States and eventually ended up in Denver, Colorado, where I completed my undergraduate and master’s degrees at the University of Colorado, and Colorado was home for more than ten years. Outside of research, I love spending time with family and friends, especially outdoors in the mountains. When I’m not outside, you will likely find me watching college football as a die-hard Oregon Ducks fan or following the other kind of football as an equally devoted Bayern Munich supporter. I am also a lover of all things classical music and opera, and few things beat dressing up on a Friday night to hear a Tchaikovsky symphony or a Mozart opera performed live.

*Note: My sole affiliation is SIMIS | Fudan University, Shanghai. My advisor, Prof. Bingjie Li, holds his appointment at Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (HUST), Wuhan, P. R. China, and all of my current research projects are joint projects between these institutions.

Projects

Current

SIMIS | Fudan University

  • Retrieval-augmented clinical LLM for cardiovascular risk prediction Developing a cardiovascular clinical large language model that learns individual disease risk from UK Biobank data, encoding biomarkers into outcome-specific risk levels with spline-based Cox models, and grounds its predictions in a retrieval-augmented library of similar patient trajectories and clinical guidelines. Large language models · retrieval-augmented generation · cardiovascular risk · UK Biobank
  • Cross-ancestry atlas of public genetic and multi-omics data Building a traceable, quality-checked atlas of public GWAS, protein QTL, and molecular QTL (expression, splicing, metabolite, methylation) data with matched LD references, spanning European, East Asian, African, South Asian, admixed American, and other under-represented populations, as a foundation for cross-ancestry comparison, fine-mapping, colocalization, Mendelian randomization, and genetic risk prediction. GWAS · pQTL · molecular QTLs · cross-ancestry · fine-mapping · Mendelian randomization
  • Global population cohort and biobank directory Building an integrated directory of population cohorts, biobanks, and health surveys across five world regions. Each resource is compared on sample size, data depth, follow-up design, and access pathway, classified into a tiered access system, and verified, so the right data source can be chosen quickly for a given research question. Cohorts · biobanks · epidemiology · data integration
  • Multi-level stacked prediction model Designing a multi-level stacked ensemble in which several base models are trained, then combined by higher-level learners, to deliver more accurate and robust disease risk prediction than any single model. Ensemble learning · stacking · risk prediction
